Beginner FAQ

How big do bearded dragons get, and how long do they live?

Adults reach about 40–60 cm (16–24 in) including the tail, usually within 1–2 years. With good care they live around 10–15 years.

What size enclosure do they need?

An adult needs at least 120 x 60 x 60 cm (4 x 2 x 2 ft). Babies grow fast, so many keepers buy the adult-size enclosure from the start.

What temperature and lighting do they need?

A warm basking spot of about 38–43°C (100–110°F), a cooler side of about 24–29°C (75–85°F), and a UVB light on for 10–12 hours a day. Replace UVB tubes about every 6–12 months, even if they still glow.

What do they eat?

Babies and juveniles eat mostly insects (crickets, dubia roaches) every day, plus some fresh greens. Adults eat mostly leafy greens and vegetables, with insects a few times a week. Dust food with calcium, and never feed wild-caught insects.

Is sand safe to use in the enclosure?

Loose sand can cause digestion blockages, especially in babies. Safer choices for beginners are reptile carpet, ceramic tile, or paper towels.

Can I handle my new dragon right away?

Give it about a week to settle in first. Then start with short, gentle sessions each day. Hand-raised dragons usually get used to people quickly.

What are signs my dragon needs a vet?

Not eating for several days, being very sluggish, swollen or bent limbs, trouble shedding, or runny droppings. Find a reptile-experienced vet before you need one.
